HOW SOFTWARE PROJECTS ARE MEASURED

Hours removed, not features shipped

We scope software against the process it replaces: what the team does today, what the system does instead, and what the difference is worth in hours and errors. On the call we walk through the closest comparable build — the manual process before, the automation after, and the time that came out of it.

Why no published numbers here? Client systems carry commercial data and the agreements are explicit. We will not publish a number a client has not confirmed — the same rule applies to our own marketing claims.

BEFORE YOU ASK

Questions on how we prove the work

Can you show results from a project like mine?

Yes, on a call. Software, CRM and marketing engagements run under NDA, so instead of publishing the numbers we walk you through the closest comparable project — the process before, what was built, and the results the client confirmed to us. Bring the system or the campaign you want to improve and we will be specific.

Why do you not publish case-study metrics on the website?

Because the ones we can publish are the ones a client has confirmed publicly, and everything else belongs to the client. We would rather have a shorter proof page and keep the trust of 24+ clients than pad this page with numbers nobody can verify.

Do you have references we can speak to?

Where a client agrees, yes — for government and institutional work this is often a formal reference on letterhead. We will tell you up front which references are available for the kind of project you are planning.

How do you report on a running engagement?

Weekly during delivery (build demo or campaign review), monthly in writing: what was shipped, what it moved, what is next. Marketing reporting is on enquiries and cost per enquiry; software reporting is against the milestones in the scope.
